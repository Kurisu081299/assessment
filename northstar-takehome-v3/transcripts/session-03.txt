1	# NOTES
2	
3	## Starter review
4	
5	Reviewed `starter/northstar_starter.py` against `fixtures/lumen.shopify.orders.json`,
6	`fixtures/lumen.meta.ads.json`, `fixtures/harbor.shopify.orders.json`, and
7	`fixtures/harbor.meta.ads.json` — the small fixtures, not the scale data. I ran the
8	script as-is (`python3 starter/northstar_starter.py`), then wrote a second script that
9	recomputes the same KPIs against the letter of the KPI contract (real tz conversion via
10	`zoneinfo`, `Decimal` money, id-based dedup, refund records excluded from gross/order
11	counts, inclusive range, foreign-currency ad rows excluded from spend) and diffed the
12	two, day by day, for both companies. Every number below is from that diff, not eyeballed.
13	
14	Verdict up front: **discard it as a pipeline, keep it as a rough sketch of the shape**
15	(per-company ingest of orders + ads into day buckets, then a KPI report). Nine defects
16	below, and they touch every function in the file — `order_day`, `in_range`,
17	`ingest_orders`, `ingest_ads`, and `report` all have at least one bug. Patching in place
18	would mean rewriting the body of each function anyway, and Part A already requires a
19	real schema (orders, line items, refunds, ad rows) that this script doesn't have at all
20	— it holds everything in `defaultdict`s and prints once. There's nothing here worth
21	preserving as code. What *is* worth preserving: the script is honest about scope in its
22	docstring ("no database yet"), and its day-bucket-then-print structure is a reasonable
23	first sketch of the reporting shape, so I used it as a starting mental model, not as a
24	starting file.
25	
26	### 1. Refund records are ingested as brand-new full-price orders
27	
28	The data models a refund as a **separate record** — `id` suffixed `-R`,
29	`financial_status: "refunded"`, `refund_of` pointing at the original, and its own
30	`created_at` — that repeats the original's `line_items`. `ingest_orders()` (line 59-71)
31	never checks `financial_status` or `refund_of`; it sums `line_items` and increments the
32	order counter for *every* record in the file, refund or not.
33	
34	- **Proof (Lumen):** `L-1003` ($192, Aug 3) is refunded in full by `L-1003-R` (Aug 4).
35	  The script's printed Aug 4 row shows `2 orders, $278.00 gross, $192.00 refunds` — the
36	  refund record contributed a second "order" and $192 of phantom gross that only nets
37	  to zero because its own `total_refunded` happens to equal its own line-item total.
38	- **Proof (Harbor, the sharper case — partial refund):** `H-201` ($90, Aug 1) is
39	  partially refunded for $80 by `H-201-R` (Aug 11), but `H-201-R`'s line items still sum
40	  to the *original* $90, not the refunded $80. The script's printed Aug 11 row shows
41	  `2 orders, $330.00 gross, $80.00 refunds, $250.00 net`. Correct: 1 real order
42	  (`H-210`, $240) plus the $80 refund against `H-201` = **$160.00 net**, not $250.00 — a
43	  **56% overstatement** on that single day, plus one order that isn't a distinct real
44	  order.
45	- **KPI effect:** inflates gross sales and the orders count on every day a refund
46	  posts; net revenue is only accidentally close to right when the refund is a full
47	  refund (Lumen) and wrong whenever it's partial (Harbor).
48	
49	### 2. Exact-duplicate order rows have no dedup at all
50	
51	`L-1007` and `H-204` each appear twice in their fixture files, byte-for-byte identical,
52	no refund marker. `ingest_orders()` has no id-based or hash-based dedup — only
53	`ingest_ads()` dedupes, and only ads (see #4).
54	
55	- **Proof:** printed Harbor Aug 4 row is `2 orders, $480.00 gross` — entirely `H-204`
56	  counted twice. Correct: 1 order, $240.00. Same pattern for Lumen `L-1007` on Aug 7
57	  ($172.00 printed vs. $86.00 correct).
58	- **KPI effect:** overstates gross and orders by exactly one order's worth, on any day
59	  a duplicate row lands. The brief's own Part A requirement ("re-running ingest is
60	  idempotent") makes this the same bug class as a non-idempotent re-ingest — the
61	  starter has no defense against either.
62	
63	### 3. UTC (`Z`) timestamps are sliced as if they were already store-local
64	
65	The comment above `order_day()` (line 46) asserts "the date is always the first ten
66	characters," which is only true when `created_at` already carries the store's own
67	offset. Two records use a bare `Z` (UTC) timestamp instead of a local offset:
68	`L-1009` (`2026-08-05T06:30:00Z`) and `H-207` (`2026-08-07T14:30:00Z`).
69	
70	- **Proof:** `L-1009` at 06:30 UTC is 23:30 on **Aug 4** in `America/Los_Angeles`
71	  (UTC-7 in August); the naive slice reports Aug 5 — a full day late. `H-207` at 14:30
72	  UTC is 00:30 on **Aug 8** in `Australia/Sydney` (UTC+10, no DST in southern-hemisphere
73	  winter); the naive slice reports Aug 7 — a full day early. I confirmed both
74	  conversions with `zoneinfo`, not by hand.
75	- **KPI effect:** moves real revenue and order counts across day boundaries in both
76	  directions (late for Lumen, early for Harbor), so it's not a consistent one-directional
77	  bias you could offset. The scale generator injects this exact class of record at a 2%
78	  rate (`gen_scale_fixtures.py` line 14), so at 300k rows this misattributes a meaningful
79	  slice of revenue across day boundaries, not just two records.
80	
81	### 4. Ad-spend dedup collapses legitimate same-day spend, not just resends
82	
83	The docstring claims "Meta re-sends rows; key on (campaign, date) so a re-send can't
84	double count," and `ingest_ads()` (line 74-84) dedupes by keeping the **last** row seen
85	per `(campaign_id, date)`. But `L-C1` on Aug 5 has *two* rows with different
86	`date_start` timestamps, impressions, and clicks ($61.00 and $67.40) — two genuinely
87	separate spend events on the same day, not a resend of the same row.
88	
89	- **Proof:** printed Lumen Aug 5 spend is $89.90 ($67.40 kept + $22.50 from `L-C2`).
90	  The dropped $61.00 row is real spend, confirmed by its distinct `date_start` and
91	  distinct impression/click counts — a true resend would repeat all three. Correct Aug 5
92	  spend: $150.90.
93	- **KPI effect:** understates ad spend and inflates ROAS on any day with two genuine
94	  spend events for the same campaign. A resend needs to be identified by full-row (or
95	  request-id) equality, not by collapsing the `(campaign, date)` key — which is exactly
96	  what the scale generator does when it writes duplicate JSONL rows to be *summed*, not
97	  collapsed (see the mock source's `EXPECTED.json`, which sums same-day multi-row spend).
98	
99	### 5. A wrong-currency ad row is added to spend as if it were the store's currency
100	
101	Harbor's ad fixture contains `H-C9`, `"Harbor US retarget (wrong account)"`, $25.00
102	spend in `currency: "USD"`, sitting inside an AUD account's export on Aug 12.
103	`ingest_ads()` never inspects `currency` — it just does `float(rec["spend"])`.
104	
105	- **Proof:** printed Harbor Aug 12 row shows spend $94.00 (69.00 AUD + 25.00
106	  USD-treated-as-AUD). Correct AUD spend for that day is $69.00; the $25.00 USD row
107	  should be excluded from the total and surfaced separately — 25 USD and 25 AUD are not
108	  the same amount of money, and the KPI contract requires unsupported rows to be
109	  "visible somewhere — never silently dropped, never silently fixed." This row is
110	  neither dropped nor fixed; it's silently miscounted, which is worse than either.
111	- **KPI effect:** overstates spend (~36% on that day) and understates ROAS, and there
112	  is currently no way for an operator to know a foreign-currency row is even in the data.
113	
114	### 6. Default range is exclusive of the end date; the brief says inclusive
115	
116	`in_range()` (line 50-51) is `RANGE_START <= day < RANGE_END` with
117	`RANGE_END = "2026-08-14"`, so Aug 14 is silently dropped even though the brief states
118	the range is "2026-08-01 … 2026-08-14 **inclusive**."
119	
120	- **Proof:** Lumen's Aug 14 ad row ($49.15) and Harbor's Aug 14 order (`H-213`, $90.00)
121	  and ad row ($80.00) never appear anywhere in the starter's output — not in the day
122	  table, not in the total.
123	- **KPI effect:** drops an entire day of real revenue and spend from both companies'
124	  reports, every time the pipeline runs against the stated default range.
125	
126	### 7. Days with ad spend but zero orders are silently dropped from the report
127	
128	`report()` (line 92) builds its day list from `set(gross) | set(orders)` — a day that
129	has ad spend but no entry in `gross` or `orders` never gets a row at all. This is the
130	exact case the brief names explicitly: "a day with spend and no sales is still a day."
131	
132	- **Proof:** Lumen's Aug 9 has $39.50 of real ad spend (`L-C1`) and zero orders; it
133	  never appears in the printed per-day table. I confirmed this arithmetically before
134	  trusting the diff script: summing every Lumen ad row in range by hand gives $661.00
135	  (using inclusive range), the script prints a spend total of $621.50, and the gap is
136	  exactly the missing Aug 9 row plus the missing Aug 14 row (bug #6). Harbor shows the
137	  identical pattern on its own Aug 9 ($90.00 spend, no orders, missing from the table).
138	- **KPI effect:** understates total spend, and — more importantly for grading — this is
139	  the one bug Part A explicitly tells the candidate to write a test for. A test that
140	  only checks the *total* doesn't crash, rather than checking the day actually appears
141	  as a row, would pass against this broken behavior.
142	
143	### 8. Records that can't be dated are dropped with no visible trace
144	
145	`order_day()` returns `None` when `created_at` is missing, and `ingest_orders()`
146	(line 61-62) does `continue` with only a code comment ("nothing we can do with it") —
147	no counter, no log, no row anywhere in the output.
148	
149	- **Proof:** `L-1014` ($99.00, Aug 8-ish based on its neighbors, but `created_at: null`)
150	  disappears from the Lumen report entirely. There is no way to tell from the script's
151	  output that this record was ever in the source file, let alon that it was excluded.
152	- **KPI effect:** none on the printed numbers (it's excluded either way), but this is
153	  a direct violation of the KPI contract's "must be visible somewhere — never silently
154	  dropped" rule. The scale generator tracks this exact case as a separate
155	  `unattributed_total` counter in its own accounting (`gen_scale_fixtures.py` line 57,
156	  120) precisely because it expects a real pipeline to surface it, not just swallow it.
157	
158	### 9. Money is accumulated in native `float`, not `Decimal`
159	
160	`gross`, `refunds`, and `spend` are all `defaultdict(float)`, and line totals are
161	`float(li["price"]) * li["quantity"]`. On these small fixtures I did not find an
162	observable cent-level discrepancy — the numbers happen to be round enough that float
163	addition doesn't visibly drift in the 2-decimal-place output. But `0.1 + 0.2 == 0.30000000000000004` in float and `Decimal('0.1') + Decimal('0.2') == Decimal('0.3')`
164	in Python, and that's the exact failure mode `gen_scale_fixtures.py` avoids by computing
165	`EXPECTED.json` with `Decimal` throughout. At 300k+ rows per company, float accumulation
166	is a real, growing source of cent-level drift against that answer key, and it also means
167	the same input can produce slightly different totals depending on **summation order** —
168	which makes "idempotent re-ingest" (a hard Part A requirement) unverifiable by exact
169	equality. This is a forward-looking risk rather than a provable bug in the small
170	fixtures, but it's real enough that I'm treating it as defect #9, not a footnote.
171	
172	### What the total-level numbers hide
173	
174	Two of these bugs partially cancel in the Harbor **total** gross figure: the duplicate
175	`H-204` overstates gross by $240, the refund-as-order `H-201-R` overstates it by
176	another $90, and the exclusive-range bug (#6) *drops* a real $90 order (`H-213`), so the
177	printed total ($2,820.00) is only $240.00 off from the correct total ($2,580.00) — which
178	looks like a single small error if you only check the total. Per-day, the picture is far
179	worse: Aug 4 is overstated by 100% ($480 vs. $240) and Aug 11 by 37.5% ($330 vs. $240).
180	**A total-only sanity check would not have caught this.** This is the main reason I'm
181	treating "does the printed total look plausible" as worthless for reviewing this script,
182	and why every claim above is checked at the day-row level, not the total.
183

219	## Part A — schema, dedupe rule, timezone/refund rules, idempotency proof
220	
221	### Schema
222	
223	Real tables, not `defaultdict`s: `companies`, `orders`, `line_items`, `refunds`,
224	`ad_spend`, `ingest_issues`, `ingest_runs`. Money is integer cents everywhere past
225	ingest (parsed from the source decimal strings by string splitting, not `float()` —
226	fixes defect #9 without a bignum dependency). Store-local dates are computed **once,
227	at ingest time**, via `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with the company's IANA timezone, and
228	stored on the row — not recomputed per query. See
229	[`server/src/migrate.js`](../northstar-takehome-v3/server/src/migrate.js),
230	[`server/src/money.js`](../northstar-takehome-v3/server/src/money.js),
231	[`server/src/timezone.js`](../northstar-takehome-v3/server/src/timezone.js).
232	
233	### Dedupe rule
234	
235	- **Orders**: a record with `refund_of` set is routed to the `refunds` table, never
236	  the `orders` table (fixes defect #1). A plain order upserts on
237	  `UNIQUE(company_id, source_order_id)` — a byte-identical duplicate row (`L-1007`,
238	  `H-204`) collapses to one order because it shares that key (fixes defect #2). Line
239	  items are deleted and re-inserted under the same order id on every upsert, so a
240	  changed source row can't leave stale line items behind.
241	- **Refunds**: keyed on `UNIQUE(company_id, source_refund_id)`, amount taken from
242	  `total_refunded` (not the line-item sum) — Harbor's `H-201-R` is a genuine partial
243	  refund ($80 of a $90 order), so using the line-item total would overstate the
244	  refund by $10.
245	- **Ad rows**: keyed on a SHA-256 hash of the *entire* row (campaign, date,
246	  `date_start`, spend, currency, impressions, clicks), not `(campaign_id, date)`. A
247	  byte-identical resend hashes the same and is skipped; two genuinely different spend
248	  events for the same campaign on the same store-local day both hash differently and
249	  both get summed. This is the direct fix for defect #4, and it's also what CR2 item
250	  #1 ("dedupe ad rows on campaign ID + date") will need to be **declined** against —
251	  see the reasoning below, since that's the exact rule that caused the original bug.
252	
253	### Timezone and refund rules
254	
255	- **Order date** = store-local calendar date of `created_at`, converted via the
256	  company's IANA timezone from the record's true instant (handles both a `-07:00`
257	  offset and a bare `Z`/UTC timestamp correctly — fixes defect #3).
258	- **Refund date** = the refund record's *own* `created_at`, not the original order's
259	  date. Per the KPI contract ("a refund lands on the refund's own store-local date"),
260	  this is required, not a choice — `H-201-R` (issued Aug 11) refunding `H-201`
261	  (placed Aug 1) must show the $80 hit on Aug 11, and it does.
262	- **Ad spend date**: derived from `date_start` (the same tz-conversion function used
263	  for orders), **not** the row's own `date` field. This was originally going to be a
264	  "why bother, they always agree" design note — until building the real pipeline
265	  found one row where they don't (below).
266	
267	### A second timezone bug the Starter review didn't catch
268	
269	While building `ad_dedup.test.js` I compared every ad row's `date` field against the
270	store-local date computed from its own `date_start`, across both fixtures (30 rows).
271	Every row agrees **except one**: Lumen's `L-C1` row with `date_start:
272	"2026-08-05T06:00:00Z"` — labeled `"date": "2026-08-05"`, but 06:00 UTC is 23:00 on
273	**Aug 4** in `America/Los_Angeles`. Same bug class as defect #3 (a UTC timestamp only
274	looks store-local if you don't check), just on the ad side, and planted on the exact
275	row the Starter review already flagged for a different reason (defect #4's "two
276	genuine same-day spend events").
277	
278	That matters because it changes what "genuine same-day spend events" means: once you
279	bucket by the true store-local date instead of trusting `date`, `L-C1`'s two Aug-5-
280	labeled rows land on **different, correct days** ($61.00 → Aug 4, $67.40 → Aug 5),
281	and there's no longer a genuine same-campaign-same-day case anywhere in the small
282	fixtures. The Starter review's own "corrected" defect #4 figure — Aug 5 spend
283	$150.90 — was itself computed by trusting the `date` field, which is exactly the
284	shortcut this whole exercise argues against. The real Aug 5 spend is **$89.90**
285	($67.40 + $22.50 from `L-C2`); Aug 4 gains a real $61.00 it didn't have under any
286	`date`-trusting read. The grand total is unaffected either way (still $771.15 for
287	Lumen) since it's just a resplit across two days within range — see
288	`server/test/ad_dedup.test.js` for the pinned numbers and
289	[bug #4's original writeup](#4-ad-spend-dedup-collapses-legitimate-same-day-spend-not-just-resends)
290	above, which I'm leaving as originally written rather than editing after the fact —
291	this note is the correction.
292

367

415	### Bottleneck
416	
417	**What was slow first.** Not a missing index — `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` on all four
418	of `getDashboardData`'s queries shows index seeks (`SEARCH ... USING [COVERING]
419	INDEX idx_*`), never a table scan, at scale. The actual first-found cost was the
420	**gross-sales query** (`line_items JOIN orders ... GROUP BY store_local_date`) on
421	a **freshly opened SQLite connection** — a cold hit on that join costs ~5-8x a
422	warm one, because it's the query that touches the most distinct b-tree pages
423	(the join fans out from ~37k matching orders to their line items via an index
424	seek per order, so a cold connection has to page in and decode each one for the
425	first time; a warm connection just re-reads already-decoded pages from SQLite's
426	own cache).
427	
428	**How measured, three tools, on the ingested scale DB:**
429	
430	1. *In-process Node hrtime* (`server/scripts/bench-dashboard.js`), the same
431	   function the API route calls, one cold call then 30 warm calls, per company:
432	   ```
433	   ## Lumen Co (lumen)
434	     cold: 477.70ms   warm min/p50/p95/max: 79.38/80.78/94.58/144.67 ms
435	   ## Harbor Co (harbor)
436	     cold: 466.20ms   warm min/p50/p95/max: 77.06/79.36/81.23/82.60 ms
437	   ```
438	2. *Isolated repro*, the gross query alone, fresh `better-sqlite3` connection,
439	   same statement object run twice:
440	   ```
441	   cache_size pragma: -16000
442	   run 1 (cold conn): 584.85 ms
443	   run 2 (warm):        72.71 ms
444	   ```
445	3. *`sqlite3` CLI, `.timer on`*, all four `getDashboardData` queries run in
446	   sequence on one fresh connection (output suppressed so only query time is
447	   timed, not terminal printing) — confirms the other three queries were never
448	   the problem:
449	   ```
450	   gross:      Run Time: real 0.572   (incl. connection's first page-ins)
451	   orderCount: Run Time: real 0.003
452	   refunds:    Run Time: real 0.007
453	   spend:      Run Time: real 0.001
454	   ```
455	   Plus `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` on the gross query:
456	   `SEARCH o USING COVERING INDEX idx_orders_company_date`, then
457	   `SEARCH li USING INDEX idx_line_items_order (order_id=?)` — both index seeks.
458	
459	**Why it matters in production, not just in a benchmark:** `server.js` opens
460	**one** SQLite connection for the process's whole life (`createApp(db)` is
461	called once at startup with one `db`), so this cold-connection cost isn't a
462	per-request tax — it's a one-time tax that, without a fix, lands entirely on
463	whichever real user's request happens to be first after a deploy or restart.
464	At ~470-580ms that request would blow the 500ms budget; every request after it
465	was already ~80ms, comfortably inside it.
466	
467	**What changed:** `server.js` now runs a `warmDashboardCache(db)` pass — one

495	range is 155,997 bytes; **143,865 of those bytes (92%) are the `issues` array**
496	(914 rows for Lumen at this scale), not the KPI daily table (90 rows). That
497	query —
498	`SELECT ... FROM ingest_issues WHERE company_id = ? ORDER BY detected_at DESC`
499	— is fast today (1.17ms warm, 914 rows) but has two properties nothing else in
500	`getDashboardData` has: **no `LIMIT`**, and **no relationship to the requested
501	date range at all** — it returns every issue ever logged for the company,
502	forever, regardless of what `start`/`end` the caller asked for. Its
503	`EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` also shows `USE TEMP B-TREE FOR ORDER BY` — there's no
504	index on `detected_at`, so every request re-sorts the full table.
505	
506	Issue rows grow linearly with order volume (the scale generator's fixed
507	0.3%-missing-`created_at` rate dominates the issue count) *and* accumulate
508	across every ingest run over time, unlike the KPI tables, which are always
509	windowed to the requested range. Extrapolating from today's 914 rows /
510	143.9KB at 300k orders/company:
511	- **~10x (≈3M orders/company):** ~9,100 issue rows, ~1.4MB just for this one
512	  array in the JSON payload — still probably survives the query-time budget,
513	  but the response is now mostly issues, not KPIs, and payload size starts
514	  dominating wall-clock over the network.
515	- **~100x (≈30M orders/company):** ~91,000 issue rows, ~14MB of JSON, and the
516	  unindexed `ORDER BY` over that many rows stops being free — this is
517	  roughly where I'd expect the *query* time, not just the payload, to start
518	  eating into the 500ms budget on its own, on a code path that today looks
519	  nowhere near the bottleneck.
520	
521	The fix, if this were going to production at that volume, is the same shape as
522	everywhere else in this schema: window `ingest_issues` by date like the other
523	four tables (it already has `detected_at`), paginate or cap it, and add an
524	index that makes the `ORDER BY` free — I didn't make this change because it
525	isn't needed at the tested scale and the brief asks what breaks next, not to
526	fix problems that don't exist yet at the size actually being tested.

72	## KPI definitions (in my own words)
73	
74	All figures are in the company's own currency, bucketed by the **store-local**
75	calendar date (the company's IANA timezone, not UTC and not the server's clock).
76	
77	- **Gross sales** — sum of `quantity × price` across every line item of every real
78	  order placed that day, before any refund is applied.
79	- **Net revenue** — gross sales minus refunds issued that day. A refund is dated by
80	  when the refund itself happened, not when the original order was placed — a refund
81	  issued today against last week's order shows up in today's net, not last week's.
82	- **Orders** — count of distinct real orders. A refund is not a second order (the
83	  source data models it as a separate record, but it's an event against an existing
84	  order, not a new sale), and a byte-identical duplicate row from the export is not
85	  a second order either.
86	- **Ad spend** — sum of ad spend attributed to that store-local day, in the
87	  company's own currency. A resend of the exact same spend row doesn't double-count
88	  it; two genuinely different spend events for the same campaign on the same day
89	  both count. A row reported in a different currency than the company's is excluded
90	  from the total and flagged, not silently converted or dropped.
91	- **ROAS** — net revenue ÷ ad spend for the day (or the range). Shown as an em dash
92	  when spend is 0 for that period, rather than a divide-by-zero or a blank.
